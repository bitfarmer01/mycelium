Mycelium: the goal

A company's knowledge should behave like an organism, not a tool. Most knowledge assistants ship as a static search box with a prompt: they answer the same way on day one hundred as on day one, they do not know what you meant when a question has two readings, and nobody can tell whether a prompt change made them better or worse. So they get used for a week and abandoned.

Mycelium is a context layer over a company's knowledge that gets better every time someone talks to it, and can prove it.

It answers with citations, spoken and on screen, and says which reading of your question it took.
It learns what you meant. Every correction becomes a prior, so the next person asking a question of that shape gets the right reading first.
It ranks what it knows by proven usefulness. A live feed shows knowledge rising when it gets cited and confirmed, sinking when it misleads or goes stale.
It rewrites its own instructions only under a test. Candidate rewrites run in isolated sandboxes against questions it has never seen, and only a gain above the noise is kept. A deliberately poisoned rewrite is rejected on screen.
It reflects. Ask what it learned today and it tells you, from its own memory.
